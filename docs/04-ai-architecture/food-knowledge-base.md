# Food Knowledge Base

Version 3.0

---

# 1. Purpose

The Food Knowledge Base (FKB) is the nutritional foundation of the Nutrition Engine.

Its purpose is to provide structured information about foods commonly consumed by GymGenius users.

Version 3.0 focuses exclusively on foods and traditional dishes from Cameroon.

Future versions will progressively expand to other African countries and eventually support global food databases.

---

# 2. Philosophy

GymGenius does not recommend foods based only on calories.

It recommends foods that are:

- locally available
- culturally familiar
- nutritionally appropriate
- affordable
- aligned with the user's health goals

The objective is to help users build sustainable eating habits using foods they already know.

# 7. Portion Sizes

The Nutrition Engine uses realistic household portions.

Examples:

- one cup of rice
- one medium plantain
- two eggs
- one tablespoon of peanut paste
- one serving of ndolé

This makes recommendations easier to follow.

# 9. User Constraints

Recommendations should adapt to:

- allergies
- dietary preferences
- vegetarian diets
- budget
- available ingredients

Unavailable foods should never be recommended.

---

# 10. Data Sources

The Food Knowledge Base should be built using:

- Cameroon food composition tables
- scientific nutrition databases
- Ministry of Public Health publications
- FAO resources
- nutrition experts

All nutritional values should be referenced and verifiable.

---

# 12. Integration with the Nutrition Engine

The Nutrition Engine uses the Food Knowledge Base to:

- build meal plans
- calculate macronutrients
- estimate calories
- suggest substitutions
- adapt meals to local availability

The AI layer may improve meal descriptions but must never invent nutritional values.

---

# 13. Future Improvements

Future versions may include:

- micronutrient information
- glycemic index
- seasonal food availability
- local market prices
- recipe preparation steps
- barcode support
- image recognition for meals

---

# 14. Guiding Principle

A nutrition recommendation is only useful if the user can realistically prepare or obtain the suggested food.

The Food Knowledge Base ensures that every recommendation is grounded in local culture, nutritional science, and practical accessibility.
